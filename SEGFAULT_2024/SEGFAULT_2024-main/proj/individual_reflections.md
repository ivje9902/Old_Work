## Ivar Jensen
I think that I'm a “fine” participant in a team. Nothing spectacular but not necessarily ruining it for everyone else. I don’t take the most active role in the team which might make it seem as though I’m not participating, but I try my best to be active in discussions and planning.

Be more active. Take on a bigger role in the team and ask more questions. I often hit roadblocks that I try to overcome on my own because I don’t want to distract the other members from their work. Also being more vocal about things I maybe don’t agree with.

The thing that motivates me in a group the most is progress. Parallel progress. When all parts are moving, everybody has a clear plan of what is expected of them, and they have full understanding of the task they must complete and in what timeframe. When everything works like a well-oiled machine. Demotivation I would say comes mostly from standstills. When the whole project is waiting on a specific piece of code or something like that.  Specifically, if the task we are waiting on is not a large task that could use more members on it.  Something else that demotivates me is when I feel that I'm dragging the team behind. When it’s my stubbornness or inability to complete a task that I know is fully withing my capabilities. Then I get demotivated.

Depending on the task my estimate will vary slightly. If it’s a task I know more about then I am confident that I can estimate a good time for it However, if the task is something I am unfamiliar with then my estimate will most likely be longer that the actual time required since I would rather be finished ahead of time than late.

I don’t have any specific role that I would like to play in a team. It more so is that I have a role that I feel fits me and as far as I know works for most group projects I’ve been in thus far.

I think I take one similar role in most group projects that I’ve been involved in. My routine for working in a group is that I most often don’t ask for any specific work. I mostly take what’s left. I think this works best in projects like these when somebody might be more familiar with a certain objective, and thus giving them that task will speed up the process significantly. I didn’t feel all that confident in what we were supposed to do and what we were supposed to do produce I kind of just took whatever work was left and started learning about that piece. Since I didn’t know that much about any of the specific tasks.

I think working in a group has its upsides and downsides. On one hand it’s really helpful when you’re stuck in the work you’re doing at the moment. Asking for a new pair of eyes can really help when overcoming tasks that might seem difficult to the individual but in reality, was easy, because you just needed a new perspective.

I don’t necessarily think I’ve learned any code related things in particular. This project felt more like a way to bring together everything we have learned over the course and show it all in a compact way. Although when it comes to coding with more than 1 other member, I would say that I’ve learned the most from the project. It was like nothing I've done before and learning to work with pull requests and scrum was both interesting and informative. When it comes to learning new things, I feel that depending on the subject I learn faster in different way. In coding it is practical work that I feel gives me the most knowledge as it is easier to understand programming by doing in my opinion.

I’ve used an excel sheet where I create a new cell every time, I start a new period of workv on the project. Whether that be individual reading before meetings, implementing some code into the project or just writing essays. Everything that I do that is connected to the project is noted in the excel sheet.

The lack of communication during the break was probably our biggest “fail” during the project. This caused some implementations to fall behind and raised the overall stress of the group.

For biggest win it would probably be our quick startup considering we began 1 week after every other group. I felt that we really quickly got started on the project once the group was finished and we worked well together in the first sprint.

The biggest problem was the feeling I got that I had nothing left to contribute to the team during the final week. My small tasks were already done but I did not feel that I could provide much assistance to the other members with their tasks. To solve this, I started writing on the essays to alleviate some of the workload from the other members.

## Erik Junsved
During the entirety of the project I feel like I've had a great presence during all virtual meetings and all but one physical group meeting, and have done my best to organize the group together, make sure rooms at Ångström are booked, and be present when questions are asked from other team members, however, when it comes to pure personal preference, I’ve not usually wanted to be much of a leader, and strived more towards doing the actual work that needs to be done, which in this case, on my end, was mostly writing code and the huge Makefile. I even became known as the “Makefile wizard”.

What has motivated me the most in this endeavor is that, as long the project isn’t too complex, it is really fun to program in C, compared to a language like Java. C makes you feel much more in control of the hardware with a lot of abstraction layers peeled off and I see it as a form of high-level assembly language. As a result of this, large refactorings in C is something that I really dislike as there is a high probability that after you project builds again after changes to the makefile, there can show up new arcane memory errors in Valgrind.

Regarding my weaknesses, I’ve managed to procrastinate work on the project during the Christmas break as a result of studying other subjects at the time. This is also a result of me being overly optimistic when it comes to time estimation. *Any* task that involves doing things you have not done before will almost always take up more time than expected, so a good method of estimating time is doing a time estimate and doubling or tripling said estimated time.

I’m really happy with the webstore integration demo implementation, something that I exclusively was responsible for, as it naturally implies an extensive test of our library, and also demonstrates something actually interactive that utilizes the refmem implementation.

The fact that I have been working in a group has made a huge difference in terms of what has been able to be done. You only need one additional member in your team to work at the same level as you and you have effectively doubled the work capacity provided that you divide your work appropriately. Unfortunately, not everyone in the group has participated to a satisfactory degree which means that there has definitely been some lost potential in terms of work capacity, which reflects what could ultimately be achieved in the end.

I feel like I’ve really learnt a lot during this project, especially regarding the subject of reference counting. It’s something I've always known existed, especially since I've been learning Rust, but never really thought too much about how it was implemented under the hood.
